# THE LAST QUESTION
## Revised debate script and panel layout

**Revised edition — 12 illustrated pages.**

**Artwork update:** The finished comic uses a closed cube throughout, following the later visual direction. Page 5 splits its first exchange into two panels. Dialogue is unchanged. The original panel directions below are retained as writing notes.

An original, non-canon John Kramer × Pinhead encounter. John is gravely ill but intellectually assured. Pinhead's anger is cold, exact and emotionless. Neither converts, confesses defeat or dismisses the other. The ending is a breath, not a verdict.

## Visual direction

One location: a bare corner of John's workshop. One steel table, two ordinary chairs. John remains screen-left; Pinhead screen-right. The opened Lament Configuration stays near the far edge of the table, visible whenever framing permits. No recorder, notebook, restraint chair, concealed apparatus, playback, victim flashbacks or table-groove gesture. John's gestures emphasise speech or manage his illness; they do not indicate secret remorse.

Start in dim neutral light. The summoning extinguishes the room's ordinary illumination beneath hard white light through architectural seams. Blacks dominate; narrow atmospheric shafts reveal dust and catch the steel. No warm redemption light at the end. Pinhead's face is pale and sharply defined; his clothing merges with the dark. John's skin remains human, exhausted and faintly clammy. The light does not diagnose a winner.

Pinhead speaks in ordinary, clean balloons. No shouting, demonic lettering or sinister grins. His anger appears as shorter answers, direct address, a fixed gaze and refusal to let an accusation pass. John speaks with considered certainty. His cough interrupts his delivery, never substitutes for an answer. Preserve modest balloon lengths and clear reading order. Split speeches at the indicated paragraph boundaries.

**Configuration continuity:** Use the original 1987 film design and opening action. Page 1 panels 2–3 form a split close-up: mechanism beginning to lift, then its completed opened position, identical camera angle. Exact hand movement, orientation and opened geometry MUST be matched to film frames before illustration. This draft reserves those two beats without inventing a twist, button, star transformation or replica display configuration. The screenplay supports sliding components and an exposed interior, but is not sufficient to certify the final filmed motion. Reference: https://imsdb.com/scripts/Hellraiser.html . No later manipulation or closing of the box.

## PAGE 1 — THE OPENING
**Five panels. Wide establishment; paired mechanism close-ups; narrow light-change beat; wide arrival.**

**1. Wide.** John sits at the bare table. An empty ordinary chair faces him. He holds the closed box. Workshop shapes recede into darkness. His posture is purposeful, not ceremonial.

SFX, small: khh.

**2. Left half of split row.** Close on both hands and the box. The original mechanism begins to lift, following the film reference. Frame enough of each hand for natural anatomy and a readable grip.

No dialogue.

**3. Right half of split row.** Same angle, box now in the completed opening position. Do not change hand ownership or mirror the ornamentation.

No dialogue.

**4. Narrow.** John has placed the opened box on the table. Cold white light cuts through the room behind the empty chair. His gaze lifts towards it.

No dialogue.

**5. Wide.** Pinhead occupies the chair. No arrival pose or flourish. His pale face is the brightest solid shape in the frame. John has not moved away.

PINHEAD: You knew what you were opening.

JOHN: I knew whom I wanted to speak to.

Small title in negative space: THE LAST QUESTION.

## PAGE 2 — THE PROPOSAL
**Five panels. Two top close-ups; wide centre; two bottom.**

**1. Pinhead, square to camera.** His gaze passes from the open box to John.

PINHEAD: You have mistaken an invitation for an appointment.

**2. John.** He meets the gaze without smiling.

JOHN: Then hear the question before deciding what brought you here.

**3. Two-shot.** A long empty strip of steel separates them. The box sits beyond their hands.

JOHN: Why do you hurt people?

PINHEAD: They seek experiences the ordinary world cannot give them.

**4. John, slightly closer.**

JOHN: That explains why they seek you. Why do you answer?

**5. Pinhead.** His head remains precisely level.

PINHEAD: Because the limits they bring are not the limits of experience.

JOHN: And if they want those limits back?

## PAGE 3 — WHO CHOOSES?
**Five horizontal panels, alternating emphasis.**

**1. Pinhead.**

PINHEAD: You arrange suffering for people who never sought you. Begin with their permission.

**2. John.** One hand opens in a restrained explanatory gesture.

JOHN: I give them a choice to survive.

PINHEAD: After taking their safety away.

**3. Two-shot.** John leans forward slightly.

JOHN: Safety can conceal what a life has become. I make the consequences impossible to ignore.

**4. Pinhead.**

PINHEAD: Yours. You make your consequences impossible to ignore.

JOHN: Consequences they can act to overcome.

**5. John in foreground, Pinhead clear beyond him.**

PINHEAD: If I force you to kneel, the choice of which knee does not make you free.

JOHN: Freedom isn't what I'm promising. A way to live is.

## PAGE 4 — THE RESULT
**Five panels. Wide top; two middle pairs.**

**1. John.** His conviction is direct, not triumphant.

JOHN: People can discover that they want their lives more than the habits destroying them.

**2. Pinhead.**

PINHEAD: How do you distinguish that discovery from terror of you?

**3. John.** A short cough into his closed hand. His next balloon follows the cough rather than replacing his response.

SFX: khh—khh.

JOHN: By what they do when the immediate danger is gone.

**4. Two-shot.**

PINHEAD: And those who remain afraid? Unable to sleep? Unable to trust another room?

JOHN: Injury does not make change impossible.

**5. Pinhead, unblinking.**

PINHEAD: Nor does change make your injury necessary.

JOHN: No. Necessity is what you have to judge before you act.

## PAGE 5 — THE JUDGE
**Five panels. Two top; broad centre; two bottom.**

**1. Pinhead.**

PINHEAD: And you appoint yourself to judge it.

JOHN: I take responsibility for the decision.

**2. Pinhead, same framing.** No expression of satisfaction.

PINHEAD: Responsibility. What consequence do you accept when your judgement is wrong?

**3. John.** Hands visible and still. He does not avert his eyes.

JOHN: The failure is mine to examine. The next decision is mine to improve.

PINHEAD: The dead do not benefit from your improvement.

**4. John.**

JOHN: No. They don't.

JOHN: But you haven't argued against intervention. You've argued that I can be wrong.

**5. Two-shot.**

PINHEAD: Enough reason to permit refusal.

JOHN: Good. Apply that standard to yourself.

## PAGE 6 — THE INVITATION
**Five panels. Alternate faces, with the box visible in the middle two-shot.**

**1. John.**

JOHN: Does opening that box mean understanding what you will do?

PINHEAD: Understanding comes through experience.

**2. John.**

JOHN: Then they cannot understand the agreement until after you hold them to it.

**3. Two-shot with the open box.** Neither reaches for it.

PINHEAD: Desire reaches beyond knowledge. They seek what they do not yet understand.

JOHN: Seeking isn't surrendering every future decision.

**4. Pinhead.** His gaze becomes more direct. No physical change in the pins or room.

PINHEAD: You speak of future decisions while setting deadlines on other people's lives.

**5. John.**

JOHN: Yes. My deadline is part of what you challenge.

JOHN: Now answer what makes your invitation permanent.

## PAGE 7 — THE AUTHORITY
**Five panels. A wide opening followed by four increasingly tight shots.**

**1. Pinhead.**

PINHEAD: What they enter is not governed by their comfort.

JOHN: Who decides that?

**2. Pinhead.** The reply is flat.

PINHEAD: An order that does not require your approval.

**3. John.**

JOHN: Neither do I require yours. We are discussing justification, not permission to question it.

**4. Pinhead.** Keep the balloon small and level. Anger without a raised voice.

PINHEAD: Do not mistake this conversation for authority over me, John.

**5. John, tired but unwavering.**

JOHN: Then stop mistaking authority for an answer.

JOHN: If you could end their suffering, what would make you choose to?

## PAGE 8 — THE PURPOSE
**Five panels. Two top; wide middle; two bottom.**

**1. Pinhead.**

PINHEAD: You assume its ending is the purpose.

JOHN: For them, it may be.

**2. Pinhead.**

PINHEAD: Their wishes do not exhaust what they can experience.

JOHN: Then whose purpose matters? Yours?

**3. Two-shot.** Pinhead gives no theatrical confession.

PINHEAD: I do not need their gratitude. You need their survival to mean you were right.

**4. John.**

JOHN: Survival gives them a future. Gratitude isn't a condition of release.

PINHEAD: Would you release someone who told you your lesson was worthless?

**5. John.** Immediate, certain response.

JOHN: If they completed the test, yes. Agreement is not the mechanism.

PINHEAD: Yet you call the mechanism a lesson.

## PAGE 9 — THE EVIDENCE
**Five panels. Three dialogue panels, a cough, then a reply held to the page turn.**

**1. John.**

JOHN: A lesson can be rejected. It still gives them something to act on.

PINHEAD: So their agreement proves your wisdom, and their rejection does not disprove it.

**2. John.**

JOHN: Their conduct matters. Whether they stop destroying themselves. Whether they choose differently.

**3. Pinhead.**

PINHEAD: A life can change after cruelty without changing because it needed cruelty.

JOHN: And pain can reveal something without giving you a right to continue it.

**4. Medium John.** A deeper cough folds him forward briefly. One hand supports him against the table. Pinhead watches, neither helping nor enjoying it.

SFX: KHH—khh.

**5. John straightens as far as he can.**

JOHN: You keep asking whether their survival proves my purpose.

JOHN: What could ever prove yours mistaken?

## PAGE 10 — THE BODY
**Five panels. Close framing, enough breathing room around balloons.**

**1. Pinhead.**

PINHEAD: A revelation need not improve a life to be real.

JOHN: Then you haven't defended the suffering. Only said something can be learned from it.

**2. Pinhead.** His eyes lower briefly towards John's chest, then meet his gaze.

PINHEAD: Your body fights for another breath. Tell me what it has learned.

**3. John.** No pause for introspection.

JOHN: My body doesn't have to learn anything.

PINHEAD: Yet you demand enlightenment from theirs.

**4. Two-shot.**

JOHN: I demand a decision.

PINHEAD: After making every other decision for them.

**5. John.** His voice is quieter from exertion, not uncertainty.

JOHN: And yours? They open a box. You call that permission.

PINHEAD: They seek an experience beyond the limits of—

## PAGE 11 — THE RIGHT TO STOP
**Five panels. Two top; wide middle; two silent bottom beats.**

**1. John interrupts.** He is answering the repeated defence, not shouting over it.

JOHN: Then they experience it. They understand what they've asked for.

JOHN: And they ask you to stop.

**2. Close on John.** A shallow breath separates the next line.

JOHN: Why doesn't that decision count?

PINHEAD, off-panel: Understanding does not undo the threshold.

**3. Two-shot.** The opened box remains between them, beyond their hands.

JOHN: Then don't call it desire after they no longer desire it.

**4. Close on John.** His next inhalation fails to fill his chest. Mouth slightly open. No speech, no symbolic vision, no supernatural constriction.

**5. Close on his hand gripping the table edge.** Show a complete, plausible hand. Tendons taut. The white light stays unchanged. Nothing moves to rescue or punish him.

No dialogue or sound effect in panels 4–5.

## PAGE 12 — THE LAST QUESTION
**Five panels. Narrow recovery strip; two middle dialogue panels; wide challenge; wide silent final image. Give the final image at least a third of the page.**

**1. Close on John.** At last, one small breath enters. His shoulders lower a fraction. He is not suddenly well.

PINHEAD, off-panel: You cling to the life that contains your agony.

**2. John.** Two separate balloons, with visible space between them. He waits for enough breath to finish.

JOHN: I want the next breath.

JOHN: That doesn't mean I want the pain.

**3. Pinhead in profile; John visible beyond him.**

PINHEAD: Neither do the people in your rooms.

JOHN: No.

JOHN: When they survive, I open the door.

**4. Wide two-shot.** Both faces readable. John's voice is thin but his eyes remain fixed on Pinhead.

PINHEAD: A door you locked.

JOHN: Yes.

JOHN: When do you open yours?

**5. Silent final panel.** Lower, close framing: John's hand at the table edge has loosened. His fingers remain naturally curved; the hand is neither offered nor extended towards the box. Include his lower chest at frame-left, caught in the small release of an exhalation. Across the table, Pinhead's pale hand remains perfectly still. The Configuration is still open in the background. The same bleak white light falls over both hands.

No answer. No caption. No smile. No closing action. End in the brief interval when breathing is possible again.

**END.**

## Editorial intent — not narration

John's illness is suffering he did not request. His wish to live is not consent to that suffering. This lets him challenge Pinhead's treatment of desire as an enduring justification, while Pinhead explicitly turns the same objection back on John's victims. John does not concede that his tests are wrong. His answer is that he provides an attainable end; Pinhead's objection is that he created the captivity. Neither claim is endorsed by the narrator.

The last question is specific: under what conditions does Pinhead allow suffering and captivity to end? The door has been verbally established as the release from John's rooms, not introduced as a mysterious prop or unexplained destination.

The final loosened grip echoes the previous page's struggle. It signifies a moment of physical relief, not remorse, forgiveness, moral improvement or acceptance of Pinhead. Readers can interpret the breath without being instructed what a near-death experience must mean.

Pinhead's silence is the boundary of the comic, not proof that he cannot answer. He remains present and dangerous. John has bought no immunity, won no supernatural bargain and ended no encounter by closing the box.

## Production checks

- 12 pages × 5 panels = 60 panels. Page 1 panels 2 and 3 are the requested split opening sequence.
- Match the box's actual opening shots before artwork. This is the only unresolved visual-reference dependency.
- Keep box orientation and opened geometry consistent thereafter.
- Keep the light bleak and cold through the final image.
- John's cough appears on pages 1, 4 and 9; breathing deteriorates through pages 10–12. It does not conveniently interrupt every difficult question.
- No narrator explains the ending inside the comic.
- No recordings, hidden tests, straps, table-groove gestures, wobbly furniture, supernatural playback or dismissal.
- John answers confidently, including uncomfortable admissions. Pinhead attacks those answers without becoming a benevolent advocate.
